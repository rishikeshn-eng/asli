Is that voice real?

Voice clones made from a few seconds of someone's WhatsApp voice note now drive “digital arrest” and family-emergency scams across India. asli is a small detector for machine-generated speech in ten Indian languages, trained on real speech against two speech engines and tested on speakers, engines and languages it never saw, through phone lines and WhatsApp compression. It is also an honest measurement of where detection stops working: it catches an unseen text-to-speech engine, but not an unseen zero-shot voice-cloning model.

The demo runs the exact model from the repository (589k parameters, ONNX) on 4-second windows. Treat the score as evidence, not proof: if a call asks for money or OTPs, hang up and call the person back on a number you know.

Why this matters

The detectors people can download don't speak Hindi.

Cloning is cheap. Open models clone a voice from ~6 seconds of audio, in Hindi, with no technical skill.
Calls are lossy. Scam audio arrives over 8 kHz phone lines or as Opus-compressed voice notes, which erases the high-frequency artefacts most detectors rely on.
Data is English. Public anti-spoofing benchmarks (ASVspoof and similar) are mostly English read speech recorded in clean conditions.

Method

Built so it can't cheat

  1. Same words, real and fake. Every synthetic clip reads a sentence a real FLEURS speaker also read, so the model can't win by recognising content.
  2. Same processing. Every clip is silence-trimmed, loudness-normalised and cut to 4 s; level and silence carry no label.
  3. Codec history neutralised. One engine's audio arrives as MP3, so 45% of real training clips go through the identical MP3 encoder. We then test real speech through that encoder (see the shortcut test above).
  4. Hard test. Speakers never seen in training, two engines never seen (Google TTS, XTTS-v2 zero-shot voice cloning), two languages never seen (Odia, Assamese), and five channels: clean, phone G.711, WhatsApp-like Opus 12 kbps, MP3 32 kbps, 10 dB noise.
